Dead Air Privacy Policy

Applies to the Dead Air and Dead Air Beta apps for Atlassian Cloud. Effective November 12, 2026.

Dead Air watches Jira Service Management desks for work that has quietly stopped, such as a request type that went silent or a recurring task that was not done. This page explains what the app stores, where it stores it, and how to have it removed.

Where your data lives

Dead Air is built on Atlassian Forge and runs entirely on Atlassian's platform. It makes no calls to any server outside Atlassian, and the app's developer does not receive copies of your Jira or Confluence content. Everything the app stores is kept in Atlassian's app storage for your own site.

What the app reads

To do its job, Dead Air reads, through Atlassian's APIs and only on sites where it is installed:

  • issues and service desk requests on desks you choose to watch, including their dates, request types and status;
  • the last-edited date of Confluence pages linked from incidents, if the Confluence part is installed;
  • whether the person watching a desk still has access to it.

Dead Air stores no people's names, email addresses or passwords. It does not store ticket descriptions, comments or attachments. The only ticket text it keeps is the short names described above: request type names, series names taken from the summary line of recurring requests, and job names you type in yourself. When it opens an alert issue on your desk, that issue lives in your Jira like any other ticket and follows your site's own retention.

App logs

Like every Forge app, Dead Air writes technical logs that Atlassian holds and the developer can read, to find and fix faults. These logs carry desk and project ID numbers, check names, counts, timings and fixed error codes. They do not carry ticket text, request type names, job names, account IDs or anything you type into the app.

Rovo agent and MCP tools

Dead Air includes an optional Rovo agent and an optional MCP server. Both are read only: they can list the desks Dead Air watches, show its results and explain why something was flagged, and they cannot change anything. They read only what Dead Air already stores and the same Jira data the board reads, with the permissions of the person asking. They run inside Atlassian, and nothing they read is sent anywhere else. The agent appears only on sites that have Rovo, and Atlassian bills any Rovo usage to that site's own plan. The MCP server is off until an admin of your site turns it on for the app.

Personal data

The only personal data Dead Air stores is the Atlassian account ID described above. Atlassian requires apps that store account IDs to report them to its personal data reporting service and to delete or update them when an account is closed or changed. Dead Air does this weekly: when Atlassian reports an account as closed, the app ends any watch held by that account and removes its account ID. You can also email the address below at any time to have a stored account ID removed.

Removing your data

Stopping a watch from the Dead Air board deletes that desk's watch record and schedules its scan history, setup choices and expected job names for deletion. Uninstalling the app removes it from your site. To ask for deletion of anything the app stored, or with any question about this policy, email 7kevinmasterson@gmail.com.
